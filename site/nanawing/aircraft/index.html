Different aircraft. More ways to grow.

The pleasure of RC flying is getting to know the aircraft: how much input it needs, when to ease off the throttle, how early to start a turn. A trainer, a nimble twin-motor wing and a fast, heavier aircraft each give you something different to work on.

Try them all. Repeat a manoeuvre, explore a new route or put your control to the test on a race course. You can make mistakes, reset and go again without a repair bill. Connect a gamepad or USB RC radio to practise on sticks, or get started with a keyboard.

01 / THE 3D-PRINTED WING · FLY WITH PRECISION

WUDFLY Spectre

Aircraft design by WUDFLY.

A plane you can 3D-print at home, recreated for you to fly here. WUDFLY's Spectre packs two motors into a 600 mm, 215 g wing. In Nanawing, its quick roll and lower top speed make it a delight to weave through a route while practising small, deliberate stick movements.

The Spectre also introduces a different way to steer: differential thrust. With a controller, the yaw stick varies the motors' speeds to swing the nose, alongside your pitch, roll and throttle inputs. Try it at a gentler pace and feel the difference. Yaw needs more than 10% throttle and a controller; keyboard flight still supports pitch, roll and throttle.

Our simulator model is an original recreation of WUDFLY's design, built from public product photos. Its handling is a simulator approximation, not a manufacturer-validated flight model.

02 / THE TRAINER · BUILD THE BASICS

Nanawing One

Original model by narenana

There is nothing quite like the moment a turn starts to feel natural. The Nanawing One gives you a welcoming place to find that feeling. Our original trainer has the lowest stall speed in the hangar and a quick roll response, so you can get comfortable holding a line, making smooth banks and working pitch and throttle together.

Once the basics click, there is more aircraft to discover. With a measured 191 km/h top speed in the simulator, the One has plenty of pace for your first race laps and the practice that comes after them.

03 / THE STEP UP · PLAN YOUR TURNS

Morok UAV

Model: “Morok UAV Drone 3D Model” by rombic992, CC BY 4.0. Rendered in Nanawing with its simulator materials.

Fly a route you know in the One, then take the Morok around it. The scenery is the same, but your timing changes. A measured 249 km/h top speed and a slower roll response invite you to look further ahead and start setting up your turns sooner.

This is where you practise adapting to the aircraft instead of asking every aircraft to fly the same way. Manage your speed, leave room for the bank and see how smoothly you can join one turn to the next.

04 / THE SPEED CHALLENGE · THINK AHEAD

Sukhoi S-70 Okhotnik

Model: “Sukhoi S-70 Okhotnik” by Muhamad Mirza Arrafi, CC BY 4.0. Rendered in Nanawing with its simulator materials.

Ready to feel what more speed does to a familiar flight? The Sukhoi is Nanawing's fastest aircraft, reaching a measured 323 km/h in the simulator, with the strongest climb of the four. It is a thrill to open up, and a very different aircraft to bring through a turn.

Its higher stall speed and slower roll ask you to manage speed and turning room with care. Practise anticipating the next move, rather than reacting at the last moment. Here, you can try again until your inputs and your flight line come together.

Where the numbers come from

A test bot flies each aircraft on the same flight model you play with. A settled full-throttle level run gives top speed, a full-stick roll gives roll rate, and a 20° full-power climb gives climb rate.

Stall speed is the unpowered, wings-level 1 g lift limit from each wing's aerodynamic curve. The in-game stall warning comes earlier, at 27, 32, 39 and 31 km/h for the One, Morok, Sukhoi and Spectre, and banked turns need more speed. The bench is rerun after any tuning, so these numbers match what you fly.

Questions

How does this help me at the RC field?

Use Nanawing to practise control inputs, throttle management and planning your turns, and to discover how different aircraft respond. A gamepad or USB RC radio lets you practise on sticks. Simulator time complements real flying; the handling is an approximation, not a substitute for instruction, aircraft checks or safe flying at the field.

Which Nanawing aircraft should I fly first?

Start with the Nanawing One if you are learning the controls. Try the Spectre for quick banks and twin-motor yaw on a controller. Move to the Morok and Sukhoi to explore how more speed changes your timing and flight planning. Every aircraft is free, so you can try them all.

Is the WUDFLY Spectre in Nanawing the real aircraft?

It's our recreation of WUDFLY's design, with the real wing's 600 mm span, twin motors and white-and-yellow livery. Its handling is a simulator approximation. The real Spectre is designed and sold by WUDFLY at wudfly.com.

Can every aircraft race every course?

Yes. Each of the four courses keeps a separate board for each aircraft, so you race pilots in the same plane. Only your best lap counts.

Do I need a controller?

No. All four fly on a keyboard, and a gamepad or a USB RC radio works too. The Spectre's yaw needs a controller's yaw stick.
